Most technology comparisons produce a winner. Almost none of them should.

A comparison between two process routes rests on inputs (energy prices,
utilisation, lifetimes, vendor performance claims) that are uncertain by
factors that are large relative to the difference being measured. Collapsing
that into "route A is better" throws away the only information the study
actually generated: *under which conditions* it is better, and how far those
conditions are from where the site sits today.

## Fix the boundary before you collect anything

The single most expensive mistake available in this kind of study is collecting
numbers before deciding what they are numbers of.

Two routes almost never have the same natural system boundary. One needs
pretreatment the other partly provides; one produces a waste stream the other
does not; one has a utility demand that the site may already be able to absorb.
Where you draw the line changes the answer, and if the line moves after data
collection has started, everything collected before the move has to be
revisited.

<Callout variant="note">
Write the functional unit and the boundary down, in a sentence each, and get
them agreed before the first email to a vendor. It feels like bureaucracy. It
is the cheapest hour in the study.
</Callout>

The functional unit needs the same treatment: not "a cubic metre" but a cubic
metre at a stated quality, delivered at a stated point, with a stated
availability. Two routes that produce the same nominal output at different
availabilities are not producing the same thing.

## Make every input a distribution

If an input is uncertain, it should enter the model as a range with a stated
shape, not as a number with a caveat in the text. Caveats do not propagate;
distributions do.

This has a useful disciplining effect. It becomes impossible to report a single
figure without also reporting its spread, and impossible to hide a wide input
behind a narrow-looking output. It also forces an explicit statement about
correlation: energy prices are not independent of each other, and pretending
they are will understate the spread in a way that is difficult to spot after
the fact.

The practical form is a Monte Carlo over the input set. The machinery is
trivial; the work is in choosing the ranges honestly and being able to say
where each one came from.

<Sidenote summary="On vendor data">
Supplier performance figures are inputs with an interest in the outcome. They
belong in the study, and they belong with a wider range than the supplier
quoted.
</Sidenote>

## Report a boundary, and an undecided region

The output that survives contact with reality is a map: over the one or two
inputs the site actually knows or controls, which route is cheaper, and, drawn
explicitly, the region where the difference is smaller than the model's own
uncertainty.

That undecided region is the most valuable part of the deliverable and the part
most likely to be edited out. It says: here, this study cannot tell you, and
the decision must be made on grounds it did not measure: risk appetite,
existing competence, what the maintenance team already knows how to fix. A
study that names the limit of its own authority is more useful than one that
does not, and it is much harder to misuse.

## Keep what you deliberately did not monetise

Every comparison of this kind leaves something out because monetising it would
be indefensible: microbiological risk, regulatory exposure, the operational
cost of a technology nobody on site has run before.

Leaving these out is legitimate. Leaving them out silently is not. They belong
in a short, explicit list at the front of the result, stated as what they are:
factors excluded from the objective that may reasonably dominate the decision.
A reader who chooses the more expensive route because of one of them has used
the study correctly, and will only be able to do so if they were told.

## The shape of the thing

Functional unit. Boundary. Uncertainty structure. Then data, and only then a
conversation with a vendor. Then a map with an undecided band on it, and a list
of what was deliberately not priced.

It is not a sophisticated structure. Its value is that it is decided in advance
and written down, so that when the numbers arrive and one of them is
inconvenient, the framework does not quietly bend around it.
